Undergraduate Research Assistant

McLaughlin Library, University of Guelph

01 Introduction

Computing in a
Student Support Setting.

I joined McLaughlin Library as an Undergraduate Research Assistant for a project exploring how artificial intelligence could support undergraduate academic skill development in learning, writing, and information literacy.

As the term progressed, my role expanded beyond AI research into data automation, workflow design, technology evaluation, user research, prototyping, and documentation across several library teams. The experience showed me how thoughtfully designed technology can support both staff and student learning.

02 Employer

The role of
McLaughlin Library.

Academic support

McLaughlin Library is one of the University of Guelph’s main academic support environments, where students can strengthen the skills they need throughout their degree. Its role extends beyond providing access to books and study space because the Library also supports students as they learn how to research more effectively, improve their writing, develop stronger study strategies, and work confidently with academic information and technology. Much of this support is delivered through consultations, workshops, learning resources, and specialized services that help bridge the gap between classroom instruction and independent academic work.

Technology & inclusive learning

The Library also plays an important role in responding to changes in how students learn and how technology is used within academic environments. As new tools such as Artificial Intelligence become more common, staff need to consider how they can support learning while also thinking carefully about accessibility, privacy, and practical implementation. This made McLaughlin Library a particularly relevant environment for my work term because my role connected academic support with emerging technology, while also giving me opportunities to contribute to projects that improved internal processes and supported the needs of different library teams.

03 My role

One role,
four work streams.

I supported the library’s digital and academic-skills work from several angles. Across each stream, I focused on work that could be reviewed, explained, maintained, and handed off clearly.

1–2/ 4

Automation & Data

For the Academic Data Collection and Reporting project, I built Python-based workflows to gather, clean, structure, and validate information from multiple sources. I added clear review points for uncertain records, helping turn repetitive manual work into a more consistent process while keeping final decisions with staff.

Tools & skills Python, Requests, BeautifulSoup, OpenPyXL, NumPy, data validation

Prompt Engineering

Through the Prompt Design and Copilot Agent Study, I created and compared prompts for classification, summaries, and structured outputs. I also studied and prototyped an AI agent in Microsoft Copilot Studio, evaluating response quality, consistency, limitations, and the review steps needed before staff could rely on its results.

Tools & skills Microsoft Copilot Studio, prompt design, agent testing, output review, documentation

Microsoft 365 development

For the Microsoft 365 Workflow Prototypes, I connected forms, lists, notifications, workbook logic, and staff-facing views into practical process concepts. I tested how information moved between tools and refined the flow to reduce repeated entry, clarify handoffs, and make progress easier for staff to track.

Tools & skills Forms, SharePoint, Power Automate, Teams, Power Apps, Excel

Research & Evaluation

In the AI and Assistive Technology Evaluation project, I researched tools using shared criteria related to accessibility, privacy, learning value, and implementation. I synthesized findings into clear comparisons that helped staff consider not only what each tool could do, but also its risks and support needs.

Tools & skills Qualtrics, Canva, Clipchamp, research synthesis, evaluation criteria

04 Goals

Goals
for the Term.

My main goal was to grow from completing assigned tasks into building thoughtful, reusable work. I wanted to become stronger at solving messy problems, communicating technical choices, and thinking about how data and AI tools affect real learning environments.

Objectives

  • Build repeatable workflows that could be checked, reused, and handed off.
  • Understand staff needs before choosing what to build or automate.
  • Evaluate AI through usefulness, accessibility, privacy, and student support.

Skills Gained

  • Technical: Python, data cleaning and validation, spreadsheet logic, Microsoft 365 workflows, prompt engineering, research synthesis.
  • Professional: clear questions, feedback-led testing, documentation, technical communication, independent work, maintainability.

Looking Ahead

These skills move me toward my goal of becoming a data scientist. The role showed me that strong data work also means understanding messy information, checking assumptions, explaining results, and creating processes that people can trust.

05 Conclusion

What I am
taking forward.

The biggest lesson from this term was that technical work is strongest when it is clear enough for other people to trust.

Across data workflows, Microsoft 365 tools, prompt engineering, and AI research, I learned that building something is only part of the job. The work also needs validation, documentation, feedback, and a careful understanding of who will use it.
