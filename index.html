01 Introduction

Technology with a
practical purpose.

From May to August 2026, I worked at the University of Guelph’s McLaughlin Library, supporting projects where technology, research, and academic support met.

My work included automating data collection, improving scheduling tools, prototyping a reporting workflow, and researching AI and assistive technologies. This report highlights what I contributed and the learning these projects supported.

02 About the employer

A library that supports
how students learn.

The library

McLaughlin Library supports learning, research, and academic skill development at the University of Guelph. I worked with internal library and academic-support teams on tools and research intended to support staff and students.

Where computing fit in

Computing was part of the service work: turning web content into usable data, checking information quality, connecting Microsoft 365 applications, and translating staff needs into maintainable tools.

03 My role

One role,
three kinds of work.

As an Undergraduate Research Assistant in AI in Academic Skill Development, my time was split across three areas: automating and preparing data, researching AI and assistive technology, and developing Microsoft 365 solutions for library staff. In each, the routine was the same. I worked out what staff needed, built or tested something, gathered feedback, and documented it for handoff.

Automation, data & prompt engineering

I turned manual, inconsistent information into structured, reviewable data. That meant writing Python automation, designing validation and cleaning steps, and engineering prompts for AI-assisted tasks, then documenting each workflow so staff could repeat it.

Projects

  • Academic program profiling A reusable Python workflow that collects program requirements, courses, and descriptions from university calendar pages, classifies requirements, and flags records for review.
  • Consultation data cleaning Standardized Writing & Learning Services consultation records, checked missing and inconsistent values, and documented the cleaning process.

What it delivered

Reviewable datasets and documented workflows that staff can reuse for future profiling and reporting work.

  • Python
  • Requests
  • BeautifulSoup
  • OpenPyXL
  • NumPy
  • Seaborn

Research & evaluation

I researched how AI and assistive technologies could support students, comparing tools against structured criteria and gathering student input instead of relying on product claims.

Projects

  • AI tool evaluation Compared academic-support tools on functionality, use cases, limitations, privacy, and implementation concerns.
  • AI policy & guidelines Reviewed university generative-AI guidance and summarized recurring themes in an environmental scan.
  • Student focus groups Supported recruitment, scheduling, and preparation, and created the materials and forms used to gather feedback.
  • Assistive technology scan Compared accessibility tools by use case, AI functionality, data processing, privacy, and retention.

What it delivered

A consistent basis for staff to compare options, along with student perspectives on AI and academic skills.

  • Canva
  • Clipchamp
  • Qualtrics

Microsoft 365 solutions development

I designed and built workflow tools on Microsoft 365, starting from how staff actually work, testing changes with them, and preparing documentation for handoff.

Projects

  • Library noise reporting A prototype connecting user reports to a staff ticket process. Staff can claim, update, reassign, and escalate reports through Teams and a customized Power Apps form.
  • Supported Learning Group scheduling An improved Excel workbook with lecture, availability, session, and room-conflict checks, plus dropdowns and conditional formatting.

What it delivered

An end-to-end prototype with implementation options and handoff documentation (a prototype, not a confirmed library-wide launch), and a scheduling workbook that highlights conflicts automatically.

  • Forms
  • SharePoint
  • Power Automate
  • Teams
  • Power Apps
  • Excel
04 Goals & learning outcomes

Goals I set,
and how they went.

Draft answers based on the work described above. To be checked against my original work-term goals before submission.

What were my goals for this work term?

I wanted the term to move me from completing tasks to building things other people can rely on. That came down to three learning goals:

  • Build data workflows that others can repeat, not one-time results.
  • Turn staff needs into tools that fit how people already work.
  • Evaluate technology on usefulness, accessibility, and privacy, not features alone.

Did I set goals tied to my job tasks?

Yes. Each learning goal was attached to specific work, so progress could be checked against real output.

  • Data workflows: the academic program profiling workflow and the consultation data cleaning.
  • Tools for staff: the noise-reporting prototype and the Supported Learning Group scheduling workbook.
  • Technology evaluation: the AI tool comparison, policy scan, assistive technology scan, and student focus groups.

What skills did I want to learn, and how will they help next?

I wanted to strengthen programming for data extraction and validation, spreadsheet logic, Microsoft 365 workflow tools, structured research, and documentation.

These carry directly into my next work experience. Reusable, documented work is easier for a team to trust, and turning a colleague’s request into something testable is useful in any software or data role.

Which technologies did I want to work with, and why?

  • Python (Requests, BeautifulSoup, OpenPyXL, NumPy, Seaborn): to pull messy web content and inconsistent records into clean, checkable data.
  • Excel logic: formulas, data validation, and conditional formatting, because staff already rely on workbooks and better logic helps them immediately.
  • Microsoft 365 (Forms, SharePoint, Power Automate, Teams, Power Apps): to connect tools staff already use into one reporting process.
  • AI and assistive tools: as research subjects, to understand what they offer students and what they cost in privacy.

Which goals did I complete, and which did I not?

Completed

Reusable, documented workflows for program profiling and consultation data. A working noise-reporting prototype and a more flexible scheduling workbook, both refined with staff feedback. Structured comparisons of AI and assistive tools that staff can use as a shared reference.

Not fully completed

Taking the noise-reporting prototype to a confirmed library-wide launch, and measuring long-term adoption or service impact. Both depend on decisions and follow-up after the term ends. I also did not connect the evaluation findings to observed use, since that needs time with the tools in practice.
